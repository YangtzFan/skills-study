# Iterative web research

Use for explicit research requests, comparisons, multi-part questions, or claims whose uncertainty requires more than a focused lookup. Apply the shared research-browser budget and artifact rules throughout. This workflow does not require additional agents or new integrations.

## Frame and allocate

Identify the user's question or decision, scope, time range, and the evidence that would change the answer. Ask only when a missing constraint would materially change the outcome; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and proceed. Briefly communicate the intended research direction for sustained work.

Maintain a small working set of subquestions, candidate sources, inspected evidence, conflicts, and unresolved gaps. Keep it in memory for an ordinary answer; persist Markdown research notes only when a durable handoff or report needs them, under the active task directory and registered in its index. Do not create a separate global research store.

Allocate the remaining calls across discovery, source reading, and verification. Start with the highest-value unknowns. Batch independent searches or reads when supported, reserving budget first; keep dependent queries and follow-up reads sequential. Explicitly deep research still uses the default limits unless the user authorizes a larger budget.

## Search, read, and revise

1. Discover sources for distinct subquestions using the search guidance. Prefer originals relevant to the actual scope, not a fixed number of links or domains.
2. Open the promising sources and inspect the supporting passages using the reading guidance. Follow material references to the original study, rule, announcement, dataset, or documentation when accessible.
3. Track each central claim with its source, exact evidence/locator, date or version, inspected coverage, and remaining uncertainty. An inference should identify which evidence supports it; a search snippet remains a discovery lead. Do not promote an unopened citation into verified evidence.
4. Identify the gap with the greatest impact on the answer. Search or read again only if resolving that gap is likely to change the conclusion and fits the shared limits. Refinements for the same subquestion retain its existing operation counter and rewrite limit.
5. For disputed or consequential conclusions, seek independent evidence and the strongest relevant counterexample. Trace shared provenance before counting multiple reports as corroboration. If sources disagree, compare dates, scope, methods, and definitions; do not silently average incompatible results or decide by source count.

For comparisons, use common criteria and comparable versions, dates, workloads, units, and baselines. Distinguish advertised capability, measured results, author opinion, and your synthesis. For academic full-text work, retain and verify papers before reading under the shared paper-download rule; metadata discovery alone is not a literature full-text review.

## Stop and deliver

Stop when the user's important subquestions are supported at the required level, further searching is unlikely to change the answer, usable capabilities are exhausted, or a shared limit is reached. Do not claim an exhaustive search from a bounded sample. Report unresolved material gaps and limit-triggered partial coverage plainly; do not reset counters to finish a report.

Lead with the answer or recommendation. Support consequential factual claims with nearby citations to inspected sources. Preserve native tool citations where available; otherwise use descriptive links to the verified public pages. Keep source facts, inference, and uncertainty clear without imposing a rigid report template. Include dates, tables, quotations, and methodology only where they help the user assess the result.

Before delivery, check that each central claim is supported by the cited passage, the passage applies to the requested scope and time, and no download or partial extraction is described as a full review. Keep quotes concise and faithful. Retain disagreements that affect the decision. If only discovery was possible, deliver candidates and that limitation rather than a simulated researched conclusion.

On follow-up, reuse the task, evidence, and provenance. Refresh only claims whose time sensitivity or changed scope warrants it. For continued research within the same task, carry forward budget counters and unresolved gaps; conversation turns do not create a new allowance.
